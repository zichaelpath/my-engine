# Devlog 031 — Bump and Click (Arc 6 Closes)

## Goal

The last two sounds of the audio arc: a knock when the player walks into something solid, and a tick when the editor selects something — feedback for collisions and for UI.

## Concept

- **A bump is a displacement deficit.** The collision system already computes the truth every frame: the player *asked* for `move` pixels and *got* fewer. Comparing intended vs actual displacement per axis detects contact with **everything** the resolver handles — crates and terrain blocks alike — without the resolver growing a reporting channel or an event system. When the deficit appears while pushing, play the knock; a 0.25 s cooldown keeps a held push from machine-gunning it (you hear *thock … thock … thock*, like testing a wall with your shoulder).
- **UI sounds borrow the game's pool.** The editor's selection tick calls the same `playSound` the footsteps use. That's a deliberate small coupling, named here: the pool is currently *Game's* audio service, and both hosts already live next to Game. If a third consumer appears, the pool graduates into its own Audio service — until then, one pool, zero duplication.
- **Both sounds are synthesized like the rest** — the bump is a pitch-dropping woody knock, the click a 2.1 kHz tick — so Arc 6's entire soundscape (steps, splash, bump, click, music, placeholder blip) is generated by one tools script from pure math. No recordings, no licences, no audio files that aren't reproducible from the repo.
- **Arc 6, in one sentence:** the engine hears now — resources cache sounds the way they cache textures, voices are pooled, gameplay sounds are edges and cadences over simulation state, music streams, and loudness belongs to the listener.

## Code built

- `Game::update`: bump detection (intended-vs-actual displacement per axis, 0.25 s cooldown) playing `assets/bump.wav`.
- Editor: selection (Game View pick and Hierarchy row) plays `assets/click.wav` through the game's pool.
- `tools/make_sounds.py`: `bump()` and `click()` generators; both WAVs committed.

## What broke

Nothing — clean build, both sounds verified in one session.

## Verification

Built clean on the Linux rig. One editor session produced the whole evidence chain in the console and counters: clicking the crate in the Game View loaded `assets/click.wav` and selected it; a diagonal drive south-east (wading — the splash fired en route) ended pressed against the crate's corner, and **`assets/bump.wav` loaded** — it only ever loads when the bump detector fires. The panel closed at **15 sounds played / 4 cached** (click, step, splash, bump — the full Arc 6 palette minus the music stream), the count consistent with ~10 steps, 1 splash, 1 click, and cooldown-paced bumps during the held push. Arc 6 is complete: every sound in the engine is synthesized, cached, pooled, and triggered by a named rule.
